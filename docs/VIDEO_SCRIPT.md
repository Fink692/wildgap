# WildGap 2:45 demo script

## 0:00–0:20 — Hook

**Picture:** Homepage hero, then UNEP quote.

“You cannot protect what you cannot see. Biodiversity data has blind spots because observations cluster where people already look. WildGap turns those missing observations into a useful field mission.”

## 0:20–0:40 — Search

**Picture:** Open Explore and search Winnipeg.

“Search any location and choose a two, five or ten kilometre area. WildGap divides it into comparable H3 cells.”

## 0:40–1:10 — Explain the map

**Picture:** Run live analysis, highlight score disclaimer and ranked list.

“We compare coordinate-valid GBIF observations from the latest year with the preceding three-year baseline. The score ranks observation coverage—not habitat health, animal abundance or population decline. Low-data regions suppress the rank entirely.”

## 1:10–1:40 — Create a mission

**Picture:** Select the top cell, show climate card and survey dates, create mission.

“Open-Meteo adds last month’s climate context and helps pick a comfortable field day. WildGap suggests an under-observed group and creates a safe, repeatable 60-minute protocol.”

## 1:40–2:05 — Proof

**Picture:** Field photo, observation evidence, completed mission, tester notes.

“We ran this Winnipeg mission for real. [Insert only verified duration, observation and evidence details.] Two people then completed the planning flow uncoached in [verified times]. Their feedback led us to [verified change].”

## 2:05–2:30 — Technology

**Picture:** Architecture diagram, mobile view, portable link in a private browser.

“The stack combines Next.js, TypeScript, H3, MapLibre, GBIF and Open-Meteo. Missions stay on-device and travel in validated portable links, so the complete experience needs no sign-in or mission database. A clearly labeled Winnipeg snapshot protects the demo from upstream outages.”

## 2:30–2:45 — Close

**Picture:** Impact counters and final CTA.

“WildGap does not promise to solve biodiversity loss from a dashboard. It helps one more person collect one more useful observation. Nature has blind spots. Let’s go find them.”
