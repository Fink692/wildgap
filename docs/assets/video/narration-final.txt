Nature data has blind spots. WildGap helps citizen scientists find where one more hour of looking could be useful.

Search any location and choose a two, five, or ten kilometre area. WildGap covers it with comparable H3 cells, then queries coordinate-valid biodiversity observations from GBIF.

It compares the latest twelve completed months with the preceding thirty-six-month baseline. A low record count is never called a wildlife decline. It is a reason to look again.

The survey-priority score is transparent: fifty-five percent density gap, thirty percent coverage change, and fifteen percent target-group gap. When the evidence is too thin, WildGap suppresses the ranking.

Open-Meteo adds recent climate context and ranks the next seven days for comfortable field conditions. Weather helps choose when to survey; it never changes the biodiversity score.

Choose a cell and generate a printable sixty-minute mission with a target group, safety checks, readiness steps, and an optional evidence link.

There is no sign-in. Missions stay on the device and travel in validated portable links. The Winnipeg demo snapshot is clearly labeled for outage-safe judging.

WildGap is built with Next.js, TypeScript, MapLibre, H3, GBIF, and Open-Meteo. This submission claims deployed product functionality and technical verification, not a completed field outing or human usability study.

WildGap. Find where nature data is missing, then go look.
